Service providers under the City’s Good Neighbor Policy have to check the area around their sites and report what they find. Before this app, that meant [paper logs, emails, calling 311 by hand]. The app turns a walk around the building into a short list of what’s left to do.

01

Getting senior leadership aligned

Who was involved. [Mayor’s Office sponsor, department directors, 311 / Public Works ops lead, provider-relations lead, legal/policy, the PM]

Compliance or service? Is the app a tool for checking up on providers, or one that helps them do the job? This set the tone: no reminders inside the app, no instructions telling clerks how to do their work, and the header shows the provider their own record.

How much to trust the AI. Should the AI file 311 tickets on its own, or should a person always confirm? The design keeps a person in the loop: filing a 311 is always an explicit button.

Which agency owns follow-up. [311 vs. SFPD non-emergency vs. the provider.] This became the To do / In progress / History split.

What changed their minds. [A walkthrough of the clickable prototype, recordings from provider sessions, duplicate-311 data, a pilot result]

The shared reference: 34 policy rules, one card each, showing leaders exactly what the AI would do for every finding.

A desk clerk already knows what to do about litter. We cut the imperative guidance from the cards. Now a card says what was found and asks one question. Two exceptions stayed: phone numbers (a fact, not an order) and safety warnings.

03

The 3-month build

Three tabs, one mental model. To do is anything you can still act on. In progress is waiting on someone else. History is settled. A ticket is never in two lists at once.

Checkbox vs. button, decided by what the tap does. On-site actions get a checkbox. 311s get a button, because the tap submits something to the City. Calls stay a button: a tick can’t say which number was called, and a call isn’t finished when it’s made. Calls cover 17 of the 34 rules, so this was the call that mattered most.

De-duplicate at capture, not later. A photo that matches an open ticket merges right away into a short receipt, “Added as an update to #P1-0033”, with one way back out.

Reuse before building. Toasts used the success / error / undo system already shipped with 311 filing. Only the informational tone was new.

Accessibility as a constraint. Text on the capture flow meets 7:1 (AAA), which meant changing two design-kit tokens.
